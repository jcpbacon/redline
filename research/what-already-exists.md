## Detail per product

**Redline (redlineapp.net)** — This is the single most important finding in this research: a live, shipping product already uses the name "Redline" and does almost exactly what this project's hypothesis describes. It is a mobile app (iOS/Android via Google Play) where a user photographs, uploads, or pastes a contract; the AI returns a 0–100 overall risk score, flags individual clauses as high-risk/caution/safe, explains each flagged clause in plain language tied back to the original source sentence, and — on its Pro tier ($89.99/year) — adds a Q&A feature over the document plus automated drafting of negotiation emails (functionally a counter-offer draft). It explicitly targets renters, freelancers, job applicants, and small-business owners, and states contracts are "never retained, and never used for training." Pricing is one-time/annual, not monthly SaaS: $9.99 for a 5-scan pack, $29.99/year for 30 scans, $89.99/year unlimited. Source: https://redlineapp.net/ and https://redlineapp.net/blog/best-ai-contract-review-apps-2026 (the product's own blog also names three other consumer-facing competitors: Justee, Contract Crab, and DocuSign Iris — those claims are unverified beyond this one source and are flagged as such in the table above).

**ToS;DR** — Community/volunteer-run, not AI-native; grades whole terms-of-service documents (A–E) rather than analyzing a user's own specific contract. Free, browser-extension based. Its limitation is coverage breadth, not accuracy per se: smaller/less-popular services often aren't graded because curation depends on volunteer labor. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terms_of_Service;_Didn%27t_Read

**DoNotPay** — The clearest cautionary tale in this space: marketed as an "AI lawyer" for consumers, it drew a formal FTC enforcement action (finalized Feb 2025) for failing to substantiate that its output matched human-lawyer quality, plus $193,000 in required consumer relief and a permanent ban on "AI lawyer" marketing claims. This is directly relevant to Redline's category: regulators have already established a bar around claiming AI-generated legal-facing output is lawyer-equivalent. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-finalizes-order-donotpay-prohibits-deceptive-ai-lawyer-claims-imposes-monetary-relief-requires

**Enterprise CLM cluster (Ironclad, Lexion, Evisort, LawGeex, Robin AI, Spellbook, Genie AI, Harvey)** — All eight of these are legal-industry / enterprise-legal-team products, sold through demo-gated sales cycles with unpublished pricing, targeting corporate legal, procurement, or law firm users who process contracts at volume (hundreds/year). None of the pricing found is a self-serve, publicly listed number — every figure in the table is a third-party estimate, not a vendor-published rate. None of these tools are positioned for an individual signing a single lease or freelance contract. Sources per-row above.

**Rocket Lawyer** — The one incumbent consumer brand with real document-review functionality (bundled with attorney consultations and template drafting), but its recurring complaint pattern is commercial/trust (billing, cancellation, upsell) rather than product-quality. This suggests consumers already have some appetite for a "review my document" product but the incumbent's reputation problem is in its business practices, not its analysis. Source: https://rocket-lawyer.pissedconsumer.com/review.html, https://www.consumeraffairs.com/legal/rocket-lawyer.html

**Consumer lease-scanner apps (Contract Analyzer AI, LeaseLogic, Lease Decoder, Lease Shield AI)** — A small cluster of low-review-count, indie-scale mobile apps doing camera-based lease/contract scanning with plain-English output, priced as cheap one-time purchases or sub-$5/month subscriptions. Review volumes are too low (5–7 ratings) to identify a genuine "most common complaint" — this itself is a finding: the consumer segment is thin enough that individual apps haven't accumulated meaningful review data yet. Sources in table above.

## Where the gaps are (evidence-backed only)

- **The consumer/renter/freelancer segment is real but thin and fragmented**, not empty. Multiple small apps (Redline itself, Contract Analyzer AI, LeaseLogic, Lease Decoder, Lease Shield AI) already exist doing camera-based, plain-English, risk-flagged contract review for individuals — evidenced by live App Store/Google Play listings found in this research. None of them have accumulated large review volumes (the two checked directly had 5 and 7 ratings respectively), suggesting no dominant consumer player has emerged yet.
- **The named-competitor "Redline" is functionally near-identical to this project's hypothesis**, including risk scoring, plain-English clause explanation with source-sentence reference, Q&A over the document, and negotiation-email drafting comparable to a counter-offer feature — evidenced directly from https://redlineapp.net/.
- **The enterprise/legal-team segment is crowded and well-capitalized** (Ironclad, Evisort, Lexion, LawGeex, Spellbook, Robin AI, Genie AI, Harvey all confirmed live with G2 listings or dedicated review coverage), all sold through opaque, demo-gated, custom pricing — none publish self-serve rates, evidenced across every G2/pricing source cited in the table.
- **Regulatory risk is established, not hypothetical**, for products claiming AI output is lawyer-equivalent: the DoNotPay FTC action is a direct precedent for anything marketed as "your AI lawyer" rather than "a tool to help you understand a document," evidenced by the FTC's own press release language.
- **Consumer trust complaints in this category skew toward billing/cancellation practices rather than analysis quality** (Rocket Lawyer's pattern), which may matter more for how Redline should structure pricing (Redline's own one-time/pack pricing avoids the subscription-cancellation complaint pattern) than for feature design.

## What I could not find

- Exact, vendor-published pricing for LawGeex, Robin AI, Ironclad, Lexion, Evisort, Harvey AI, Genie AI, or Spellbook — every number in this report for those products is a third-party estimate, not a quoted list price; several sources materially disagree with each other on Spellbook's per-seat cost.
- Direct-quote user complaints for Robin AI, LawGeex, Genie AI, or the Redline app itself — the G2 pages for Robin AI, Spellbook, and Lexion, plus the FTC's full complaint document, returned HTTP 403 and could not be fetched directly within the tool budget; the Google Play reviews section for Redline did not render in the fetch. Complaint data reported for those products comes from secondary summarizing articles, not the primary review platform.
- Any Hacker News or Reddit thread specifically discussing "using ChatGPT to read my lease/contract" — search results surfaced general commentary and third-party blog framing of the behavior, not an actual forum thread with user quotes, despite this being explicitly requested in scope.
- Luminance, Legartis, Detangle, ClauseBase, and Loio were checked only lightly; ClauseBase in particular appears to be a document-automation/template tool rather than an AI clause-review tool and may not belong in a head-to-head competitor list at all — this was not independently confirmed beyond one search snippet.
- Whether LawGeex and Evisort still operate as standalone products — one 2026 industry-guide source claimed they "no longer operate as standalone products," implying acquisition or shutdown, but this was not corroborated by a second source.
